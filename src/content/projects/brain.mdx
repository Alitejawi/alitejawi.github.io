## Problem

I save a lot: articles, research papers, case studies, talks and videos. Saving was easy. Finding the right thing again weeks later was not. Bookmarks pile up, titles don't say what's inside, and a lot of the useful content is spoken in a video or written on a slide, where search can't reach it.

## What I built

A pipeline that reads what I save, so I don't have to remember where I saw it.

- **Takes in** articles, research papers, case studies, videos and posts.
- **Reads what search can't:** speech is transcribed on the Mac with Whisper, and text on slides is read with Apple Vision.
- **Local models** (Ollama) write a summary and tags for every save with enough text to summarise.
- **`/ask`** answers a question from my saved notes, and cites the sources it used.

## How I measured it

Search quality is measured against a fixed set of 50 test queries, so every change is compared on the same questions. A local model wrote each query from a saved note, as a stand-in for how I would half-remember it. It is not a study of real searches.

Before and after the search rebuild, both scored on 9 November 2026:

| Metric | Before | After | Gain (95% range) |
|---|---|---|---|
| hit@5 (the right note in the top 5) | 42% | 68% | +26 points (10 to 42) |
| hit@1 (the right note first) | 22% | 40% | +18 points (2 to 34) |

At hit@5, 17 queries went from missed to found and 4 went the other way. With only 50 queries the ranges are wide, and the hit@1 gain is only just clear of zero.

The rebuilt search combines keyword search (BM25) with vector search on a new embedding model. On 12 November I scored each method on its own:

## Guardrails

- **No summary from too little text.** If a source has fewer than 400 characters of text, Brain skips the AI summary instead of guessing.
- **Missing saves raise an alert.** A daily check compares what I saved with what arrived, and alerts my phone about anything missing.

## What I changed after launch

Brain started on n8n in April 2026. On 18 June, a security change to the SSH bridge that n8n used for transcription began rejecting every command. n8n recorded each failure as data, so its runs kept finishing as success: 676 of them in the last two weeks alone. For ten weeks no video or reel transcript reached Brain. Articles kept arriving, which hid the gap, and all eight of my watchdog checks passed. I found out by accident on 29 August, when I searched for something I had saved and it wasn't there.

The checks were testing stand-ins for success: a job ran, a binary existed, an exit code came back. None of them tested the outcome. I replaced them with checks on what actually arrived, then rebuilt the pipeline as a tested Python package and retired n8n on 4 September 2026. All 176 items in the pipeline's ledger from before that cutover are still there. As of November 2026 the package has 1,476 tests and 524 commits.
